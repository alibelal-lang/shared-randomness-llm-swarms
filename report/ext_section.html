How this relates to earlier work

Citations checked on 8 November 2026 against publisher or arXiv records.

Which convention a population settles on

  • Kandori, Mailath & Rob 1993 and Young 1993, Econometrica. With rare random mistakes, a finite population ends up at the risk-dominant convention, and leaving a convention gets rarer as the population grows. Our size effect is this pattern with LLM policies. It also explains why the stag hunt, where the worse option is the safe one, got stuck about four times as often.
  • Robson & Vega-Redondo 1996, J. Economic Theory, and Bergin & Lipman 1996, Econometrica. Which convention wins depends on how players are matched and how noise varies across states, not only on payoffs. Coupled sampling changes exactly that noise structure while leaving each agent's own choice probability fixed.
  • Effective population size, from population genetics. Correlated reproduction makes a population drift like a smaller one. Our coupled sampler sets the variance to that of 4 agents, which is why it helps only where small populations do better.

Conventions among people and LLM agents

  • Centola & Baronchelli 2015, PNAS, and Centola et al. 2018, Science. Human groups form shared conventions without central coordination, and a committed minority of about 25% can overturn one.
  • Ashery, Aiello & Baronchelli 2025, Science Advances. LLM agent populations form conventions spontaneously and show collective biases no single agent has. We instead measure each model's frozen policy once and simulate many populations from it, which separates the model from population effects and allows a controlled intervention.
  • Akata et al. 2025, Nature Human Behaviour. LLMs played self-interested repeated games well and coordination games worse. Here the coordination failure is collective: it grows with population size while each agent's policy stays fixed.
  • Hammond et al. 2025, "Multi-Agent Risks from Advanced AI". Names miscoordination among agents with shared interests as a core failure mode; lock-in at the worse convention is a small measured case.

Many copies of one model

  • Kleinberg & Raghavan 2021, PNAS, and Bommasani et al. 2022, NeurIPS. Decision-makers sharing one algorithm can lower welfare and give the same people the same outcomes everywhere. In our games, correlation across copies is neither good nor bad in itself: it amplifies whatever a small group would do, escaping the worse convention in most stuck variants and losing the better one in a few.

Measuring LLM agents reliably

  • Sclar et al. 2024, ICLR. Meaning-preserving format changes swing accuracy by up to 76 points. Our wordings moved stuck variants from 1 to 10 of 40.
  • Zheng et al. 2024, ICLR, and Pezeshkpour & Hruschka 2024, Findings of NAACL. Models prefer some answer options regardless of content; Zheng et al. trace this mostly to the option letter. In our two-option prompts the reverse held: list position moved choices by up to 0.86, the letter by at most 0.18.
  • Chen, Zaharia & Zou 2024, Harvard Data Science Review. GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 behaved very differently three months apart. Adaption-hosted models moved beyond noise on about 40% of prompts within hours, with no version reported.
  • He / Thinking Machines Lab 2025. Identical requests can return different outputs depending on server load. With precision and default sampling settings, this is one plausible source of the provider and retest differences we measured; we did not isolate the cause.
